I started coding in 2001. Back then a "big release" meant a CD-ROM in the post and a weekend of praying.

Now a new frontier model lands and we all refresh our feeds before the kettle's boiled.

This week Anthropic released new Claude models ahead of its IPO. Tech stocks wobbled, then bounced back. Same week, a lot of folks asking if AI is a bubble.

Honestly? I don't know. Nobody does.

But here's a story. Tina runs a bakery. A shiny new oven arrives, twice as fast as the old one. Everyone in the street comes to look.

Tina's first batch comes out lovely. Her second batch is burnt. Not because the oven is bad. Because she stopped checking the bread.

That's the part people forget. A faster oven doesn't fix a missing process. It just helps you make mistakes quicker.

Same with AI and code. The tools get better every few weeks. The tests, the code review, the "does this actually do what we think it does" bit? That's still on us.

I've seen plenty of hype cycles. The tools change. The fundamentals really don't.

Anyone else leaning harder on tests since the AI tools showed up? Or is it just me?
