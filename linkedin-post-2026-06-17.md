# LinkedIn Post — June 17, 2026

SpaceX just bought Cursor for $60 billion.

Let that satisfyingly absurd number sink in for a second.

Four days after the biggest IPO in stock market history ($85.7B raised, $2T+ valuation), Elon used that freshly minted stock to snap up the AI coding tool that half of Silicon Valley already can't live without.

Cursor hit $2.6B in annualized B2B revenue. A code editor. Making more than most SaaS unicorns ever dreamed of.

Here's what I think this really signals:

The AI wars aren't about chatbots anymore. They're about who owns the developer workflow. SpaceX merged with xAI earlier this year, and now they're betting that the company that builds the best AI-powered engineering tools wins everything downstream — rockets, autonomy, infrastructure, all of it.

The biggest acquisition of a VC-backed startup. Ever. And it's for a text editor on steroids.

If you're building software today and not thinking about how AI tooling is reshaping who gets to build what — and how fast — you're already behind.

What's your take — genius move by Musk, or peak AI bubble?
