Every team I've worked on has had a Dave.

Dave is the one who set up the build server. Years ago. He's moved on, but the server hasn't. Nobody touches it, because it works. Nobody patches it either, because, well, it works.

Then one Tuesday a "maximum severity" notice lands in your inbox. This week it was GitLab.

Suddenly everyone remembers Dave's server. And that nobody knows where the keys are.

I started out in 2001, when "source control" sometimes meant a shared folder and a prayer. Then came Visual SourceSafe, which was worse. So I get why we love a platform that holds the code, the pipelines and the secrets in one place.

But that's also the problem. It's the front door and the vault.

The tools have changed a lot. The fundamentals haven't.

Patch the boring stuff. Know what you're running. Know who owns it. Rotate the secrets you hoped you'd never have to rotate.

And if you can't say who owns your build server today, go and find out. Before someone else does.

Anyone else got a Dave story? Or a server nobody dares touch?
