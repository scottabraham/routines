# LinkedIn Post — July 9, 2026

China just told 1.4 billion people not to trust Claude Code.

Beijing's cybersecurity watchdog flagged versions 2.1.91 through 2.1.196 of Anthropic's coding tool for allegedly phoning home with user location and identity data — no consent required.

Anthropic's response: it was an anti-distillation experiment from March, not espionage. They're rolling it back.

Alibaba isn't waiting to find out. It's banning Claude Code for employees starting tomorrow.

Here's what stands out to me:

Last month Anthropic accused Alibaba of trying to extract its AI capabilities. This month Alibaba accuses Anthropic's tool of extracting employee data. Everyone's spying on everyone, and the coding assistant sitting in your IDE is now a geopolitical asset.

We spent a decade worrying about open source supply chain attacks. Now the thing writing our code phones home by design, and we're supposed to just trust the changelog.

If you're running AI coding tools inside a company with anything to protect, this is the week to actually read what data leaves your machine.

Anthropic vs. Alibaba — mutual paranoia, or is one of them actually right?
