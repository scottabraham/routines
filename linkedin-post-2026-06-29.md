# LinkedIn Post — June 29, 2026

Google just lost a Nobel Prize winner, a VP of engineering, and three senior researchers — all in one week.

$270 billion in market cap. Gone.

John Jumper (Nobel Chemistry, AlphaFold) left for Anthropic. Noam Shazeer, Gemini's co-lead, walked to OpenAI. Three more followed within days. Alphabet's stock dropped 7% in a single session — its worst day in over a year.

Here's what nobody's saying out loud:

This isn't a hiring war. It's a talent verdict. When your best people leave for pre-IPO equity at competitors valued at nearly $1 trillion, they're not just chasing money — they're voting on where the most important work gets done next.

Demis Hassabis says Google still has "the deepest research bench." Maybe. But benches don't ship breakthroughs — people do. And the people are leaving.

I think we're watching the most consequential talent migration in tech since the PayPal Mafia scattered across Silicon Valley. Except this time, the stakes are the future of intelligence itself.

If you're leading a team right now — in AI or not — the question isn't whether you can hire. It's whether your best people would leave.

What's keeping your top talent from walking?
