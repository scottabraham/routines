**Source story:** GitHub Octoverse 2026: TypeScript leads, ahead of Python and JavaScript; Rust is the fastest-growing language (TechRepublic, Nov 2026)

Back in 2001 I was writing JavaScript in Notepad.

No types. No linting. Just me, a 17" CRT and an alert() box telling me something was "undefined". Very helpful. Thanks.

So this week's news made me smile.

GitHub's 2026 Octoverse report puts TypeScript at number one. Ahead of Python. Ahead of plain JavaScript.

Let me tell you about Tina and Bob.

Tina writes TypeScript. Bob doesn't believe in types. "Slows me down," says Bob. He's been saying it since 2014.

Bob ships fast. Really fast. Then he spends Thursday afternoon working out why a user's name is coming through as [object Object].

Tina's code told her about that bug on Monday. Before she'd even saved the file.

Now add AI coding agents to the mix. They write quite a lot of code. Basically all day. They don't get tired and they don't get bored.

But they do guess.

Types turn those guesses into something you can check. The compiler becomes the colleague who quietly says "are you sure about that?" before anything reaches production.

And that's the part people forget.

Types were never really about the code. They're about trust. Trusting your teammates' code. Trusting the new starter's code. And now, trusting the code your agent wrote at 3am.

Bob's started using TypeScript, by the way. Don't tell him I told you.

Any old-timers here who held out longer than Bob? What finally changed your mind?
