## The post

Meet Bob.

Bob is a bouncer at a very busy club. He's been on the door for years. Checks every ID. Never misses one.

Then the club gets ambitious. "Bob's great, but he's slow. Let's hire Tina. Tina can check sixty-four IDs at once."

And she can. She's brilliant. Everyone loves Tina.

Except nobody ever checked what Tina does when someone hands her an ID with a dodgy little smudge right at the end.

Turns out she waves them straight past the rope. And into the stock room.

That's basically what happened in Java this week.

It's been nicknamed Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938). It's a heap overflow in the JVM's hardware-accelerated Base64 decoder, the AVX-512 fast path. Feed it a malformed string with bad "=" padding at the end and it writes about 62 bytes past the end of the buffer.

Base64. Of all things.

I've been decoding Base64 since roughly 2001. It's one of those things you just stop thinking about. It's plumbing. It works.

And that's the part people forget. The scariest bugs usually aren't in the shiny new code. They're in the stuff everyone trusts. Especially the "optimised" version of it.

The other bit that got my attention: the researcher reportedly found it in about an hour, auditing OpenJDK with an AI coding agent, for under $200 in tokens.

An hour.

Twenty-five years ago this would have been someone with a stack of printouts, a highlighter and a lot of coffee. Probably for weeks.

I don't find that scary, honestly. I find it quite encouraging. But it does mean the bar has moved, for the good guys and the not-so-good guys.

So, the practical stuff:
→ Running Java 17, 21, 25 or 27 on x86 servers? Patch. Oracle's fixed builds came out on 17 Nov.
→ Can't patch today? -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics turns off the fast path. Expect a small slowdown.
→ ARM64 reportedly isn't affected.

The moral? Test your edge cases. Especially the boring ones. Especially the fast ones.

Bob would never have let that smudge through.

Any other old-timers had a "but that's just Base64…" moment this week?
