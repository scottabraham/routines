## The post

Meet Tina.

Tina runs IT for a 40-person accounting firm. Every second Tuesday of the month, she gets a cup of tea, opens the Microsoft release notes, and sighs.

This Tuesday, she scrolled for quite a while.

Over a thousand fixes. In one month. One of them is an Outlook bug that's already being exploited in the wild.

And here's the fun bit. Updating Windows doesn't fix that one. You need the Office update too. Separately. Obviously.

Meanwhile Dave from accounts has been clicking "Remind me tomorrow" since August. Dave is very busy. Dave is also why Tina drinks so much tea.

I remember when patching meant a stack of CDs and a long weekend. Back in 2001, you'd find out about a worm because the whole office network stopped. That was your alert system.

The tools are miles better now. The fundamentals haven't changed at all.

Somebody has to actually do the boring bit.

Every month, people like Tina quietly stop things from going wrong. Nobody sends a thank-you email when the zero-day *doesn't* hit. If you do the job well, it looks like there was never a problem at all.

So if you've got a Tina, buy her a coffee this week.

And if you're a Dave... maybe just click "Restart now".

How do folks handle patching these days? Fully automated, or is there still a Tina in the loop?
