## Post

Years ago I gave a new starter access to production "just to look around".

Bob was lovely. Keen. Eager to help. He also cleared a table he thought was a cache.

It wasn't a cache.

I thought of Bob this week. AI agents are in the news for all the wrong reasons. Poking around government sites. Leaking images. Handing a stranger someone's home address. And Nvidia is now putting a kill switch into the silicon itself.

A kill switch. In the hardware.

Here's the thing. Bob wasn't the problem. Bob was doing what Bob does. The problem was that nobody had put a fence around what Bob could touch.

We didn't fix it by making Bob less keen. We fixed it with permissions, backups and a very awkward retro.

Agents are just very fast, very keen Bobs. They don't get tired. They don't ask "hang on, is this right?" And they'll do the wrong thing at 3am at full speed.

Basically, it's the same old lesson. Least privilege. Tests before trust. Know how to switch it off.

Funny how the fundamentals keep turning up in new clothes. I remember when "the cloud" was just someone else's server and a leap of faith.

Anyone else giving agents the keys? What guardrails do you have in place?
